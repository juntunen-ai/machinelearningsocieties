3 November 2026 - Machine Learning Societies

Anthropic moved the compute bet onto public markets, at 27 times revenue

Signal 5/10 · Stakes 10/10

Anthropic filed an amended prospectus on 2 November. It reports $895.4 billion of purchase commitments and leases, almost all for computing, against $33.24 billion of revenue in nine months. The filing moves the largest capital bet in the industry onto public shareholders, and it makes the supply contracts the number that now sets the pace of deployment.

Signal

On 2 November Anthropic filed an amended Form S-1 with the Securities and Exchange Commission. The form is the registration document for its listing on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT. The company states an aggregate committed spend of about $895.4 billion in purchase commitments and lease agreements. It says that substantially all of this spend is for computing and infrastructure. A further $175 billion of contracts are not yet priced. The total therefore reaches about $1.07 trillion.

Observed. Three numbers set the scale. Revenue for the nine months to 30 September was $33.24 billion. The commitments are 27 times that figure. Payments run to 2045, and $69.8 billion falls due within twelve months. The company holds $131.9 billion in cash and short-term investments. Growth is also slowing as the commitments arrive. Third-quarter revenue was $17.0 billion, 47 % higher than in the second quarter. The two earlier quarters grew 144 % and 132 %. Computing costs reached 58 % of sales. The company reports a third-quarter operating loss of $567 million and a net loss of $23.29 billion, most of it a non-cash charge from convertible notes and derivatives.

Caveats. A purchase commitment in a registration statement is a ceiling, not a bill. Much of the $895.4 billion depends on capacity that suppliers have not built. The company can renegotiate, delay or cancel parts of it. The comparable figure from a year ago is not public, so this document cannot show how fast the commitment grew. The document is also written to sell shares. The size is disclosed; the firmness is not.

Assessment. The document changes who carries the risk. Until last week a small group of private funds carried the buildout. After the listing, index funds and pension savers carry part of it, at a price that is set in public. The commitments also name the beneficiaries. Broadcom holds $161 billion of equipment leases, Google $121 billion through 2033, Amazon $110 billion, and SpaceX up to $120 billion. Four suppliers now hold contracts that a share price must support. For a reader outside the industry, the change is this: the electricity bill, the memory price and the chip order book now depend on a quarterly earnings number. For that to change a household's decisions, the shares must reach pension portfolios at scale and then fall. The stakes are 10 because this listing changes the structure of the compute market itself: it sets a public price for capacity years before the capacity earns anything.

Evidence 5/10.

  • P. The amended Form S-1 filed with the SEC on 2 November is a primary document. 3 points.
  • N. One newsletter sender, The Innermost Loop, carried the $895.4 billion figure and named the filing as its source. 1 point.
  • L. The figures reached X and the tier-one press before the newsletters. 1 point.

The sum is 3 + 1 + 1 = 5.

Stakes 10/10. A listing of this size changes who can enter the compute market and at what price, and that is a change in market structure.

Thesis

The binding constraint on frontier artificial intelligence has moved from chips to the cost of capital. Anthropic has signed fixed obligations that run to 2045 against revenue that arrives one quarter at a time. The company must therefore report to two audiences at once: customers who buy tokens, and shareholders who price a twenty-year supply chain every three months. The first audience forgives a delay. The second does not. From now on, the pace of deployment is set by the price of a share, and the price of a share responds to a growth rate that has already fallen from 144 % to 47 % in two quarters.

Antithesis

The strongest objection is that every large build looks over-committed at the start. Railways, canals and mobile networks all signed supply contracts that looked reckless until demand arrived. A purchase commitment is not a debt payment, and much of this one can be rescheduled. Gross margin is about 85 %, revenue rose roughly twelvefold in a year, and $131.9 billion of liquid assets covers the $69.8 billion due in twelve months almost twice. If revenue doubles twice, the commitment falls to about seven times revenue without a single contract changing. On this reading the filing is a disclosure event, not a solvency event, and a listed lab with ninety pages of risk factors is more accountable than a private one.

Forecast

By 30 June 2027, at least one European pension supervisor — EIOPA, the UK Pensions Regulator, or Finland's Finanssivalvonta — publishes a data request or guidance that names listed artificial-intelligence infrastructure as a concentration risk (40 %). Resolution date: 30 June 2027. Scoring rule: a hit requires a published document that names the exposure; otherwise the forecast misses.

Political consequences

One to two years. Instrument: index-inclusion rules and the disclosure guidance of the SEC's Division of Corporation Finance. Actor: S&P Dow Jones Indices and the SEC. Observable: a published decision on index entry for ANT, or a comment letter that requires the issuer to state its commitments against revenue in its discussion of results.

Three to four years. Instrument: pension prudential rules, including the prudent-person principle in IORP II and the code of the UK Pensions Regulator. Actor: EIOPA, the Pensions Regulator and Finanssivalvonta. Observable: a supervisory statement that sets a concentration limit, or a separate reporting line for exposure to artificial-intelligence infrastructure.

Market test: mixed, and the pro-business effect dominates. The commitments route demand to four named suppliers and two named underwriters. Ownership widens, which is pro-market. Entry for a new supplier of computing does not get cheaper.

What to watch

Three observables. The pricing of the $175 billion of unpriced contracts in the next amendment. The first quarterly report after the listing, and whether the commitment line falls. The first pension fund that discloses a position above 1 % of assets. A fourth sits on the supplier side: memory prices, where long-term agreements now cover most of the 2027 and 2028 capacity.

How the Signal is scored

Every edition prints two numbers, and they are read together. The first measures the quality of the evidence. The second measures how much changes if the evidence holds.

  • N — independent confirmation. One point for each newsletter or newsroom that adds a fact of its own. Repeating a press release adds nothing.
  • L — first publisher. One point when the party at the centre of the story published it first.

A story with a primary document, one independent outlet that adds a fact, and the actor as first publisher scores five. The same story repeated ten times without a new fact still scores four.
