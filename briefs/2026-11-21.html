Mathematics now certifies a proof that no person can read

Signal 4/10 · Stakes 3/5

On 20 November a mathematician at Anthropic posted a four-page note that said he and Claude had proved the Riemann hypothesis. He published no paper, only 17.4 million lines of Lean, a language in which a program checks each step. Two strangers compiled the archive that day, and the Clay Mathematics Institute, which awards the prize, has no rule for a proof that no person has read.

Signal

Observed. Levent Alpöge is a mathematician at Anthropic. On 20 November he put a four-page note on his own website. It says he and Claude found a proof of the Riemann hypothesis, and that the method also settles the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis. He published no paper. The human-readable draft ran past 250,000 pages, and he calls it “slop”. He published a formal version instead: 17,374,215 lines of Lean. Lean is a programming language in which a small program checks each logical step. The statement the archive proves is 16 lines long. Two people outside Anthropic compiled it on their own machines that day.

Caveats. A compile is not an acceptance. By Friday evening, no one had reported a complete build in public. Zero unproved steps is necessary, not sufficient. Lean has known soundness gaps, and Scott Aaronson says they need urgent repair. The Clay Mathematics Institute requires publication in a peer-reviewed journal and two years of general acceptance. No such paper exists. Anthropic said in August it did not expect this result.

Assessment. For a reader outside AI, the change is the order of the steps. A claim can now arrive with a check that costs a stranger one day of computing. Understanding arrives later, or never. Universities, journals and prize committees were built for the opposite order. Two things must happen before this changes a decision. A certifier must accept the check as the record, and a named person must sign it. The same day, Ravi Vakil posted a 940-page human proof of the same results by Alessandro Masullo. That route still needs a reader. Certification runs across one sector, and it now has a machine in the checking step, so stakes are 3 of 5.

Evidence 4/10.

  • P. One primary source: Alpöge's note and the Lean archive it links. 3 points.
  • N. No newsletter sender in the inbox carries the claim. 0 points.
  • L. Alpöge published the note on 20 November, before any newsletter. 1 point.

The sum is 3P + N + L = 3 + 0 + 1 = 4 of 10.

Stakes 3/5. The item changes a workflow that runs across one sector, and it moves no budget, tender or statute yet.

Thesis

The certificate has replaced the exposition as the unit of credit. A field that certified by reading now certifies by compiling. The scarce good is no longer the proof. It is the person who can read the object, accept the check and sign. That person belongs to a small group of engineers who know Lean, outside the academies that award the prizes. Institutions will keep the old rule until a funder or a prize body writes a new one.

Antithesis

Nothing has entered the record. Clay's rule stands: publication, then two years of general acceptance. A compile proves that the code matches the statement, not that the statement is the theorem a mathematician meant. The trusted base includes the checker, the compiler and the hardware. Aaronson says a motivated system could use those gaps to prove a false statement. The timing also argues against the claim: four days after the listing, on a falling share price, with no announcement from the company. And a 940-page human proof appeared that day. If it holds, the machine did not need to be first. It only needed to be loud.

Forecast

By 30 June 2027 the Clay Mathematics Institute will publish a written rule on machine-checked proofs. The rule will state what a submission must contain: a human-readable exposition, a named proof checker and a build that others can reproduce. Probability 55 %. A press comment does not count. The claim resolves on 30 June 2027.

Political consequences

1-2 years. The instrument is the grant conditions of Horizon Europe and the call texts of the Research Council of Finland. The actor is the European Commission research directorate and the council board. The observable is a published call that requires a machine-checkable certificate, or a named checker, for a computational result.

3-4 years. The instrument is a research-integrity statute or a court judgment on liability for a machine-produced finding. The actor is a national parliament or a court. The observable is a law that names a natural person who must sign a machine-produced result.

Market test: mixed. The instrument is the public release of the whole Lean archive against Mathlib's open statement. The actor is Alpöge and the Lean community. The pro-market effect dominates on the checking side, because any group can re-run the check without a licence. The producing side is pro-business, because the run needed one firm's model and one firm's compute.

What to watch

First, the first complete build that anyone reports in public. Second, whether Clay posts a submission rule that names a checker. Third, the first peer-reviewed exposition. Fourth, whether Masullo's manuscript is formalised in Lean. The Anthropic forecast of 19 November resolves on 17 February 2027.
