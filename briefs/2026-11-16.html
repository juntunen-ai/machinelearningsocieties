OpenAI's own blog now certifies a physics proof

Signal 7/10 · Stakes 6/10

A company now hosts the page that announces a physics result, and no referee stands in between. OpenAI opened a science blog on 15 November. The first guest post, by the physicist Nathaniel Craig, says GPT-6 Astra took the lead on the detailed proof of a candidate lattice definition of the Standard Model. The preprint went to arXiv on 6 October.

Signal

Observed. Nathaniel Craig leads the particle theory group at the University of California, Santa Barbara, and works at OpenAI as a visiting scientist. His post says the result is a candidate nonperturbative definition of the Standard Model on a lattice. A lattice replaces continuous spacetime with a grid of points. On a finite grid a physicist can define a theory without treating its interactions as small corrections.

The preprint is arXiv:2610.09093, "A gauge-invariant measure for lattice chiral fermions", submitted on 6 October. It carries a Lean formalisation and supplementary calculation records. Craig writes that he had a strategy but could not prove it in every case it had to cover. GPT-6 Astra, he writes, "took the lead in developing the detailed proof".

Three lattice theorists, Maarten Golterman, Andreas Kronfeld and Yigal Shamir, read the proof and asked questions, according to secondary coverage. The post calls the result "much more a beginning than an end".

Caveats. The physics is a candidate. Reflection positivity at finite spacing stays open, and so does the interacting theory in continuous spacetime. The construction covers four-dimensional Euclidean lattices and gauge fields that meet a condition the paper calls admissible. Craig calls it "an answer in principle rather than an answer in practice".

The order matters. The preprint came in October and the post in November, so priority sits on arXiv, where any reader can check it. But no journal has accepted the proof, the three reviewers come from secondary coverage, and no referee report is public. No compute figure or working time is published either, so no outsider can test the claim that the model did the work.

Assessment. For a reader outside AI, one thing changes. The first public authority for a hard scientific claim moved from a journal to a company page. The claim may survive review, and that is not the point. The lab now sets the terms on which the public meets the result, and it decides how much of the work belongs to the model.

Two events would change the picture. A journal must accept the preprint after review. A funder must write a rule on how to credit and audit a model's contribution. Neither exists today.

Evidence 7/10.

  • P — 3 points: the OpenAI science post of 15 November and the arXiv preprint of 6 October.
  • N — 3 points: AGI Pulse (the arXiv number and the three reviewers). Superpower Daily (the Lean formalisation; the problem stood open for more than 25 years). AI Briefing (the link to the Yang-Mills result).
  • L — 1 point: OpenAI published first, on its own channel.

The sum is P 3 + N 3 + L 1 = 7/10.

Stakes 6/10.

A sector has started to move the place where it certifies a result to the seller's channel, and no rule has changed yet. A published disclosure standard would make this 7.

Thesis

A laboratory can put a hard claim in front of the public without a referee, and the public has no cheap way to see what the model did. That is the delegation problem. A person signed the post and a model led the proof. The accountability chain runs to the person, and the credit flows to the company. Science gains a fast channel and loses a filter.

Antithesis

The strongest objection is that nothing institutional changed. The preprint was public on arXiv five weeks before the post. Three specialists read the proof and questioned it. Computer algebra has helped mathematicians for forty years, and a proof assistant is not a co-author. On this reading the post is communication, and calling it certification confuses marketing with governance.

The objection is strong and it does not settle the case. A blog post reaches further than a preprint, and the lab that hosts the post also chooses which reviewers it names. The weak point is that nobody outside can measure the model's share of the work.

Forecast

Horizon: through 30 April 2027. By then arXiv or a major physics publisher will publish a written policy that requires authors to state a model's contribution to a proof, with a named standard for that statement. Probability: 55 per cent. Resolution date: 30 April 2027. Score YES if a policy page or an editorial names model contribution to proofs as a disclosure item. PARTIAL if the rule covers text alone.

Market test — pro-business. Instrument: the corporate science blog as a first-party channel. Actors: OpenAI and the labs that will copy it. Who gains: the lab, because its claim reaches the public in its own words, with no fee and no review.

Political consequences

1-2 years: Instrument: the authorship and disclosure guidelines of research funders, such as the Research Council of Finland and Horizon Europe; actors: the boards that write them; observable: a disclosure clause that names a model's contribution to a proof.

3-4 years: Instrument: a liability rule for AI-assisted results in publicly funded research; actors: ministries of science and university employers; observable: a rule that assigns accountability to a named human author, whatever the model did.

What to watch

  • Whether a journal accepts arXiv:2610.09093 after review, and what its report asks about the model.
  • Whether another lab opens a science blog within six months. A second channel makes the shift structural.
  • Whether anyone publishes the compute or the working time behind the proof.
How the Signal is scored

Every edition prints two numbers, and they are read together. The first measures the quality of the evidence. The second measures how much changes if the evidence holds.

Signal, 0–10: the evidence. The formula is E = 3P + N + L, and the maximum is ten.

  • P — primary source. Three points when the claim rests on the actor's own document, announcement or statement. Zero when it rests on reporting about the actor.
  • N — independent confirmation. One point for each newsletter or newsroom that adds a fact of its own. Repeating a press release adds nothing.
  • L — first publisher. One point when the party at the centre of the story published it first.

Stakes, 1–10: the consequence. 1 is one team's experiment. 3 is one workflow in one organisation. 5 is a workflow adopted across a sector. 7 is a procurement rule or standard that changes. 9 is a statute, a liability rule or a supervision rule. 10 is a change in market structure. The even steps are the cases between them.
