The agents finish less than half the work

Signal 5/10 · Stakes 5/10

Stanford HAI published results from two benchmarks built to test agents end to end inside working environments: a simulated electronic health record and a set of payer portals. Frontier models completed under half of the tasks, even as they pass more than ninety per cent of narrow, single-step clinical questions.

Signal

Observed. PhysicianBench and HealthAdminBench give an agent an environment, a goal and tools, then check what changed: prior authorisations, appeals and denials, equipment orders. MedAgentBench, measuring single tasks, went from under seventy per cent completion in 2025 to over ninety per cent in 2026. The end-to-end benchmarks did not follow.

Caveats. Simulated environments, synthetic patients, and tasks designed by the benchmark authors. A completion is not a clinically acceptable completion, and the gap may narrow quickly once agents are tuned to these environments.

Assessment. The distance between a good demo and a finished workflow is where budgets now sit. Buyers who believed the demo will fund integration instead of pilots. Stakes 5: a workflow across a sector, not a rule.

Evidence 5/10.

  • P 3. The HAI publication of 19 October, with the benchmark design and results.
  • N 1. One health-IT newsletter that reported the benchmarks and their limits.
  • L 1. Stanford published before the newsletters; the account posted the result first.

3 + 1 + 1 = 5.

Stakes 5/10. A workflow adopted across a sector: administrative work in health systems is the same shape everywhere, so a result here moves buying behaviour across hospitals rather than in one.

Thesis

Diffusion is limited by the last mile, not the model. The work that remains, checking what changed in the environment, is precisely the work institutions are unwilling to hand over without a person watching.

Antithesis

Benchmarks understate deployment: production systems are narrower, better instrumented and wrapped in rules, so half a benchmark may still be a profitable product.

Forecast

By 31 October 2027 at least one US health system reports an agent handling more than half of its prior authorisations with human sign-off (50 %).

Political consequences

1–2 years. Instrument: insurer rules for prior authorisation. Actor: CMS and large payers. Observable: a published rule on automated submissions or review.
3–4 years. Instrument: reimbursement conditions. Actor: CMS and EU health insurers. Observable: payment tied to audited agent performance in administrative workflows.

What to watch

Whether the benchmark leaderboard moves when agents are tuned for the environment, and the first payer that publishes agent-handling rates.

How the signal is scored: Evidence E = 3P + N + L, maximum ten, where P is a primary source, N counts distinct newsletter senders that add a fact, and L marks the primary party posting first. Stakes C run from 1, a single team's experiment, through 5, a workflow adopted across a sector, and 7, a procurement rule or standard changing, to 10, market structure changing.
