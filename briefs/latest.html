AI's next constraint is the lender, not the chip

Signal 6/10 · Stakes 10/10

In November the market repriced the companies that build AI. Nvidia lost 38 % of its value in the month, and the chip index lost 21 %. The next limit on new data centres will come from the price of debt, not from the supply of chips.

Signal

Observed. Nvidia closed at $125.53 on 20 November. The price fell 14 % that day. It is 38.4 % lower than on 1 November. The Nasdaq fell 5.3 % on the same day. It is 11.1 % below its level on 1 November. The Nasdaq and the S&P 500 now sit more than 10 % below their recent highs. Traders call that a correction. The SOX index of chip makers fell 21.2 % in the month. Anthropic priced its shares at $83 on Thursday. The listing raised the largest sum in history and valued the firm at $1.5 trillion. The shares closed at $88.91 on Friday. Reuters reported that the SEC examines Nvidia's accounting. The firm had raised its revenue outlook for 2027 in the same week.

Caveats. A share price does not measure use. Orders, deployments and signed power contracts outlive a correction. The fall starts from a high base: Nvidia traded above $220 in May. The immediate causes are personal and legal. The chief executive resigned, the regulator opened an inquiry, and a report alleges a diversion of chips to China. None of these is a measured fall in demand. One week of trading is not a trend, and the month included a midterm election.

Assessment. For a reader outside AI, the change is in the schedule. A data centre is an asset with a life of 15 to 20 years. Its owner pays for part of it with debt. When the equity that backs the loan halves, the lender asks for more. Projects without a signed tenant move first. Two things must happen before this changes a decision. Lenders must widen the margin on data-centre debt, and a named project must stop. The change touches the terms on which the sector raises money, so stakes are 10 of 10.

Evidence 6/10.

  • P. One primary source: exchange prices for 20 November and the published terms of the Anthropic offer. 3 points.
  • N. Three senders add a fact of their own: Axios AM, Last Week in AI and Marcus on AI. 3 points.
  • L. No tier 1 account posted before the newsletters. 0 points.

The sum is 3P + N + L = 3 + 3 + 0 = 6 of 10.

Stakes 10/10. The item changes market structure: it decides who can finance new capacity, and on what terms.

Thesis

The binding limit has moved from chips to credit. The buildout assumed that the shares would keep rising. A 38 % fall in the supplier's equity changes the collateral and the mood. Capacity will fall where the balance sheet is weakest: independent developers and the new cloud firms. It will not fall where the model is weakest. The state becomes the lender of last resort, through guarantees, offtake contracts or a regulated return.

Antithesis

The largest buyers do not fund the buildout from their share price. Microsoft, Google, Amazon and Meta spend from cash flow. A 38 % fall in one supplier's equity does not stop them. Demand signals rose: Nvidia lifted its 2027 outlook. Anthropic still raised the largest sum in history, so capital exists at a price. The scandal is specific to one firm. A resignation and an accounting inquiry say little about whether firms buy inference. A lower price is not lower use. Deployment data must fall first, and it has not.

Forecast

By 31 March 2027 at least one named United States data-centre developer will cancel or delay a project of 300 MW or more, and will name the cost of finance as a reason. Probability 60 %. A delay for permits or a grid connection does not count. The claim resolves on 31 March 2027.

Political consequences

1-2 years. The instrument is the large-load tariff and the integrated resource plan at a state utility commission. The actor is the state commission, and FERC for the cost of transmission. The observable is a written order that moves network upgrade costs to data-centre customers, or that rejects a load contract.

3-4 years. The instrument is supervisory reporting or a capital rule for AI infrastructure credit. The actor is the Federal Reserve and the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, and the European Banking Authority in the EU. The observable is a named exposure line for data-centre lending in supervisory data, or a stress test that includes it.

Market test: pro-business. The instrument is vendor finance and private credit, tied to multi-year purchase commitments. The actor is Nvidia and its creditors. A firm with cash and signed offtake gains, because a new entrant now needs a balance sheet as well as a model. Entry becomes more costly, so the dominant effect is pro-business.

What to watch

First, the December decision of the Federal Reserve and the yield on ten-year Treasury notes. Second, the margin that lenders charge on data-centre debt. Third, the first named project that slips. Fourth, Nvidia's next report and its capital spending guidance for 2027. Fifth, the outcome of the SEC inquiry. The forecast of 27 October resolves on 31 January 2027.
