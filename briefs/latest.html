The test that decides which agent gets the work is private property

Signal 4/10 · Stakes 6/10

SemiAnalysis published GDPx on 2 November: 206 tasks in 11 domains, from chip design to legal work, built with 14 data partners. The best model, Claude Opus 5.5, scored 12.0 %, and every model scored zero on the finance and legal tasks under binary grading. The test that now ranks agents for buyers is a private asset.

Signal

Observed. On 2 November SemiAnalysis published GDPx, a benchmark for agents. A benchmark is a fixed set of tasks that compares systems, and an agent carries out a task over several steps. GDPx has 206 tasks in 11 domains: chip design, cybersecurity, biology, finance, legal work, quantitative trading, recursive self-improvement, hardware design, materials science, alignment and software engineering. Fourteen data partners supplied the tasks, and each task runs in a simulated workplace. The default limit is eight hours. The headline score is pass@1 over three attempts: the share of tasks solved on the first try, averaged over three runs. The 11 domains carry equal weight. Claude Opus 5.5 leads at 12.0 %. Gemini 4 Carbon follows at 10.9 % and GPT-6 Astra at 9.6 %. On the finance and legal tasks every model scored zero under binary grading, where a task counts as right or wrong.

Caveats. The tasks are private, and no outside group has checked them. The authors say safety classifiers cut the scores of Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra on cyber, biology and alignment, so a refusal counts as a failure. Equal weights let one weak domain pull down a strong model, and binary grading turns partial work into zero. SemiAnalysis sells subscriptions and invites partners for the next version, so the publisher also sells access to the test.

Assessment. For a reader outside AI, the number answers the buyer's question: can an agent finish a day of professional work? On this test it cannot. A bank needs one number for its model-risk file, and GDPx supplies it. Its owner then controls the tasks, the grading and the release date. A buyer's decision changes when a regulator can audit the tasks, and when the score separates refusal from inability. Stakes are 6: every sector that buys models already pays for evaluation, and this test aims to take that budget line. No rule requires it yet.

Evidence 4/10.

  • P: 3 points. The publication itself gives the task count, domains, partners and leaderboard.
  • N: 0 points. No independent newsletter in this morning's sample added a fact. Two aggregators repeated the release.
  • L: 1 point. SemiAnalysis posted the result on X at 02:06 UTC, before the aggregators carried it.

Three plus zero plus one is four.

Stakes 6/10. Evaluation budgets are now a market, and a private test that becomes the reference sets who can enter.

Thesis

The agent economy needs a yardstick, and the yardstick is now a product. Whoever owns the test sets the price of entry. A laboratory that scores badly must buy the next evaluation, and a buyer trusts one number instead of a staff. GDPx sells to both sides. Its first reading is that agents cannot yet do the work that carries the economy, and that reading will move budgets before any rule.

Antithesis

A benchmark is a machine for the answer its designers can grade. Equal weights across 11 domains reward breadth, not the depth a bank needs. Binary grading hides partial work, and an average of three attempts hides variance. The data partners sell the environments they supplied, so a hard task raises their value. A safety classifier turns refusal into incapacity. Change the rubrics and the same models may score 40 %. The number measures a two-day-old test.

Forecast

By 30 June 2027, at least one of the 14 GDPx data partners, or a named bank, insurer or law firm, publishes a vendor-selection or model-risk document that cites GDPx. Resolution date: 30.06.2027. Scoring rule: the claim hits if such a document is public and names GDPx. It misses if no public document cites it by that date. Probability: 55 %.

Political consequences

One to two years. The instrument is model-risk guidance that supervisors already enforce, such as SR 11-7 in the United States and the ICT rules for EU banks. The actor is a bank's model risk committee, or a supervisor that asks for the validation file. The observable is a published model inventory that names an external benchmark for agents.

Three to four years. The instrument is the power to evaluate models under Article 92 of the AI Act, or a public framework for buying evaluation. The actor is the European AI Office, with a national market surveillance authority. The observable is a public tender for tasks that test agents, or a published evaluation that uses tasks the vendor does not own.

Market test: pro-business. The instrument is the data-partnership licence. The actors are SemiAnalysis and the 14 partners. They gain a paid position where buyers choose a model, and entry needs proprietary task data, not a better model.

What to watch

Does GDPx publish task-level scores and rubrics? Does a bank or a regulator cite it? Does version 2 keep the same domains and weights? The forecast on the Impagliazzo group's verdict on OpenAI's refutation of the exponential time hypothesis resolves on 31.01.2027, inside 90 days.

How the Signal is scored

Every edition prints two numbers, and they are read together. The first measures the quality of the evidence. The second measures how much changes if the evidence holds.

Signal, 0–10: the evidence. The formula is E = 3P + N + L, and the maximum is ten.

  • P — primary source. Three points when the claim rests on the actor's own document, announcement or statement. Zero when it rests on reporting about the actor.
  • N — independent confirmation. One point for each newsletter or newsroom that adds a fact of its own. Repeating a press release adds nothing.
  • L — first publisher. One point when the party at the centre of the story published it first.

A story with a primary document, one independent outlet that adds a fact, and the actor as first publisher scores five. The same story repeated ten times without a new fact still scores four.

Stakes, 1–10: the consequence.

  • 1 — one team's experiment.
  • 5 — a workflow adopted across a sector.
  • 7 — a procurement rule or standard changes.
  • 10 — market structure changes.

The anchors are steps, not a scale of effort: 3 is a change inside one organisation, 9 is a rule that binds most of a market. A low signal with high stakes is a thin claim about a large thing, and it is marked as such.
