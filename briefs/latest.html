China licenses the agent, and the licence covers every update

Signal 5/10 · Stakes 9/10

China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology published Notice No. 42 on 6 November. The notice makes phones, tablets and wearables with a preinstalled agent pass new tests before they can connect to a Chinese network. It also makes the device maker file each agent update with the ministry, which may review the update before users receive it.

Signal

Observed. Notice 42 of 2026 comes from the ministry's Information and Communications Administration. It is signed 24 October and published 6 November. It rests on Chinese cyber, data and telecoms law, including the rules on network access for equipment. An agent, it says, perceives, remembers, decides, interacts and acts on its own. An agent terminal is a phone, tablet, wearable or other telecom terminal with an agent preinstalled. Four duties follow. Agent developers must test each agent against each device model and report the result to the app platform before listing. Device makers must add an agent adaptation test to the network access test and run a fraud risk assessment. A maker that sends an agent update over the air must give the ministry a plan first and a report after the release. The ministry then asks experts and takes comments, and only after that may the maker serve users. App distribution platforms must publish the adaptation list, refuse downloads to incompatible models, and remove agents that fail the test. Agents on terminals must carry a unique identity code, set with reference to the national standards for agent interconnection. The notice also orders a trusted identity system and management platform for agents.

Caveats. The notice is an administrative document under telecoms rules, not an AI statute. It binds agents that ship preinstalled on terminals. An agent reached through a browser or a web app stays outside its text. The identity code points to standards that the notice says are still being written. The notice sets no fine; enforcement runs through the licence and the platform duty. The expert review of every update can become a formality, or a queue. No single incident prompted the notice.

Assessment. For a device maker, the notice adds a compliance file to every launch and a review step to every update. For an app platform, it adds a police duty: check the list or lose the agent. For a user, it adds a promise that the agent has a name and a tested scope. The decision changes when the first update waits for review, or when the first platform removes an agent. Stakes are 9 because the notice changes who supervises an agent in the largest device market, and it acts before the agent runs, not after it fails.

Evidence 5/10.

  • P. Notice 42 of the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, signed 24 October 2026 and published 6 November 2026. 3 points.
  • N. Ctrl+AI+Reg reported the notice on 9 November. It named the ministry document and added the identity requirement and the Singapore comparison. 1 point.
  • L. The ministry published on 6 November, three days before the newsletter. 1 point.

The sum is 3 + 1 + 1 = 5.

Stakes 9/10. The notice puts a new approval step between an agent update and its users, across every phone, tablet and wearable sold in China.

Thesis

China regulates the version, not the model. A model can change without a new licence. An agent update on a licensed device cannot. The state joins the release process at the point where capability reaches the user. Britain gives ministers power to investigate an agent that escapes control. China gives a ministry power to stop the agent before it ships. Both answers point the same way: the permission is the object of regulation.

Antithesis

The strongest objection is that a telecoms notice is not an AI law. It binds devices, not agents in general. A vendor can move the agent into the browser and out of scope. The identity code waits for a standard, so the passport may be a sticker. Pre-approval assumes that the ministry can judge a model faster than the vendor can train one. If it cannot, the queue becomes the policy, and Chinese users receive older agents. A state that approves each version also owns each failure.

Forecast

By 30 April 2027 the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, or one provincial communications administration, will publish at least one rectification or delisting notice against an app distribution platform that listed agents without adaptation verification (60 %). Resolution date: 30 April 2027. Scoring rule: the notice must appear on miit.gov.cn or on the site of a provincial administration. Press coverage alone does not count.

Political consequences

One to two years. Instrument: the network access test and the over-the-air filing procedure in Notice 42. Actor: the ministry and the provincial administrations. Observable: a published list of approved agent terminal models, or a handset launch delayed for a missing verification.

Three to four years. Instrument: the trusted agent identity system and the national standards for agent interconnection. Actor: the ministry, with the cyberspace administration. Observable: a public registry of identity codes that a distributor must check before it lists an agent.

Market test: pro-business, and that effect dominates. Instrument: pre-approval, plus the platform duty to delist. Actor: the ministry. Domestic device makers and the large app platforms gain the gatekeeper role. A foreign agent provider pays the adaptation cost. Entry becomes dearer, not cheaper.

What to watch

The ministry's policy interpretation, published the same day. The first update that waits for review, and whether the ministry names it. The standard for identity codes. And Singapore's test of the domain name system, which would issue the passport without a ministry.

How the Signal is scored

Every edition prints two numbers, and they are read together. The first measures the quality of the evidence. The second measures how much changes if the evidence holds.

Signal, 0–10: the evidence. The formula is E = 3P + N + L, and the maximum is ten.

  • P — primary source. Three points when the claim rests on the actor's own document, announcement or statement. Zero when it rests on reporting about the actor.
  • N — independent confirmation. One point for each newsletter or newsroom that adds a fact of its own. Repeating a press release adds nothing.
  • L — first publisher. One point when the party at the centre of the story published it first.

A story with a primary document, one independent outlet that adds a fact, and the actor as first publisher scores five. The same story repeated ten times without a new fact still scores four.

Stakes, 1–10: the consequence.

  • 1 — one team's experiment.
  • 5 — a workflow adopted across a sector.
  • 7 — a procurement rule or standard changes.
  • 10 — market structure changes.

The anchors are steps, not a scale of effort: 3 is a change inside one organisation, 9 is a rule that binds most of a market. A low signal with high stakes is a thin claim about a large thing, and it is marked as such.
