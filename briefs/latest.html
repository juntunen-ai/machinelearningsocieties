27 October 2026 - Machine Learning Societies

OpenAI convened the referees, and that is the result that will last

Signal 4/10 · Stakes 6/10

On 26 October OpenAI said its models had produced proofs of three long-open problems, in harmonic analysis, complexity theory and quantum field theory. It published two with machine-checked formalisations and gave the third to a physics group that will write its own account. The proofs may not survive. The arrangement for checking them is new, and it is the part that will outlast the claims.

Signal

Observed. OpenAI's note of 26 October reports three results. In harmonic analysis, the model proved Sogge's local smoothing conjecture, open since 1991, in every dimension, which OpenAI says also settles the Kakeya conjecture. In complexity theory, it gave a deterministic algorithm that exactly counts the satisfying assignments of fixed-width Boolean formulas in subexponential time; for 3-SAT that refutes the Exponential Time Hypothesis, an assumption behind hundreds of hardness results. In physics, it proved the existence of four-dimensional interacting quantum field theories with gauge fields and fermions, quantum chromodynamics included. Each proof was rewritten in Lean, a proof assistant that checks every step against fixed rules. OpenAI used three release routes. The mathematics went out at once as raw material, as in its 6 October release of 722 manuscripts. For computer science it published the proof and the Lean file, with an independent group led by Russell Impagliazzo and Ramamohan Paturi of the University of California, San Diego, and Ryan Williams of MIT. The group wrote that its involvement "should not be taken as certification", asked for all raw data and took no payment; OpenAI agreed and will fund a conference. For physics the materials are withheld, and a group led by David Gross will write the account itself. OpenAI also gave 5 million dollars to the Simons Foundation for workshops on machine-produced results.

Caveats. Lean checks form, not meaning. A formalisation that encodes a weaker statement passes the checker and settles nothing. The physics claim cannot be examined, because the material is held. Refuting the hypothesis removes a hardness assumption; it delivers no faster algorithm for any problem in production. The three groups are unpaid, but the party under examination chose them and holds the artifacts. No result is in a journal.

Assessment. For a reader outside AI the change is a price. Producing a plausible proof of a hard theorem now costs less than checking one. The checking lands on universities, journals and funders, none of which holds a budget line for it, while the lab holds the only fund that pays. That inversion is the news, not the theorems. Stakes are 6: the validating workflow of a sector now has a named budget line, but no rule has changed.

Evidence 4/10.

  • Primary source = 3: OpenAI's research note, the published write-up and Lean files, and the verification group's open letter.
  • Independent confirmation = 0: No newsletter in the inbox had added a fact of its own by publication time.
  • First mover = 1: OpenAI and the verification group published before the press did.

3 + 0 + 1 = 4.

Stakes 6/10. Verification is now a funded workflow inside one sector, with named money and named convenors, but no formal rule binds anyone yet.

Thesis

The scarce good has moved from the proof to the certificate. When a machine can generate plausible arguments faster than a field can read them, the authority to say "this is settled" becomes the expensive part, and whoever convenes the certifiers sets the terms on which machine knowledge enters public reasoning. OpenAI has done that once. It can do it again.

Antithesis

This is a press release with referees attached. Three mathematicians cannot certify a field's results, and an unpaid group chosen by the lab is a committee, not an institution. Lean shows that a file compiles, not that a theorem is true in the sense the field cares about. Announced breakthroughs usually shrink. If the hypothesis falls, it will fall through years of use by people who never join a shepherd group and never get paid. The durable effect may be that one company now appears to own the referee layer, which is worse than having none.

Forecast

By 31 January 2027 the group led by Impagliazzo, Paturi and Williams will publish a public written verdict on the refutation, stating whether it holds, holds only for restricted formulas, or fails. Resolution: the group's public output on 31 January 2027. Probability 70 %. Base rate: a named group with raw data and funding has every reason to report, but claims of this size often shrink.

Political consequences

1–2 years. Instrument: grant conditions at research funders, including the Academy of Finland and Horizon Europe. Actor: research councils and the European Commission. Observable: a call that requires applicants to disclose machine-produced results and to describe how they were checked. Market test: pro-market if the rule is public and applies to every provider; pro-business if access is negotiated lab by lab.

3–4 years. Instrument: a rule on credit and liability for machine-produced results, written into funder regulation and journal policy. Actor: ministries, universities and learned societies. Observable: a rule naming who must verify such a claim before publication and before it enters a public decision. Market test: pro-business, unless the certifying body stays independent of the lab that pays for it.

What to watch

  • Whether the Lean statement matches the conjecture as the field defines it. This is the only test that matters.
  • Whether the physics group publishes, and whether its version converges with the model's.
  • Whether any journal accepts a machine-produced proof without a new review process.
  • Whether a funder writes a disclosure clause into a live call before 31 January 2027.
How the signal is scored

Two numbers are given for every edition, and they are read together. The first measures how good the evidence is. The second measures how much changes if the evidence holds.

Signal, 0–10: the evidence. Written as E = 3P + N + L and capped at ten.

  • P — primary source. Three points when the claim rests on the actor's own document, filing or statement. Zero when it rests only on reporting about the actor.
  • N — independent confirmation. One point for each additional newsletter or outlet that adds a fact of its own. Reprinting the same press release adds nothing.
  • L — first mover. One point when the party at the centre of the story is the one that published it first.

A story carried by the actor's own document, confirmed by one independent outlet that adds a fact, and published first by the actor scores five. The same story repeated by ten outlets that add nothing still scores four.

Stakes, 1–10: the consequence.

  • 1 — a single team's experiment.
  • 5 — a workflow adopted across a sector.
  • 7 — a procurement rule or standard changing.
  • 10 — market structure changing.

The anchors are steps, not a scale of effort: 3 is a change inside one organisation, 9 is a rule that binds most of a market. High signal with low stakes is a well-evidenced event that does not travel. Low signal with high stakes is a thin claim about something large, and it is labelled as one.
