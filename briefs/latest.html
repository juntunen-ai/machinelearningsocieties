26 November 2026 - Machine Learning Societies

The chip rule existed for eighteen months and controlled nothing

Signal 3/10 · Stakes 9/10

The Biden administration published a global licence rule for advanced AI chips in January 2025, due to take effect that May. Days before it would bite, the incoming administration said it would not enforce it. The rule was formally rescinded and replaced only on 16 November 2026. Reuters reported on 25 November that capacity grew in countries such as Singapore, where Chinese companies rented computing power to train and deploy models.

Signal

Observed. The replacement is an interim final rule from the Bureau of Industry and Security, the export agency inside the Commerce Department. A data-centre customer in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia or Brazil now needs a United States licence to buy an advanced chip. An existing overseas customer needs one too, if it supplies significant volumes of computing power to a company headquartered in China, Russia or Cuba. The rule also rescinds the earlier global requirement.

The Commerce Department said in early 2025 that it would not enforce the earlier rule, and for eighteen months it did nothing. The bureau wrote the replacement, but the agency said the Treasury and the National Security Council directed it.

Caveats. No filing states how many chips crossed, at what price, or which model they trained. Reuters describes a mechanism, not a measured quantity. A licence governs the next sale; it does not reach capacity already installed. Physics narrows the channel: a frontier run needs tens of thousands of accelerators joined by fast interconnect for weeks, so rented capacity replaces a domestic cluster only when that cluster is large.

Assessment. A rule that shaped the market existed, and nothing enforced it. The instrument that decides how far machine learning spreads is a licence list, written by the Treasury and the National Security Council, not by the technical agency. The stakes reach 9 because supervision of an export class changed, and five countries are bound at once.

Evidence 3/10.

  • P: 1 — the interim final rule, and the Reuters account of the gap on 25 November.
  • N: 0 — no monitored newsletter added a fact or named its own source.
  • L: 0 — the primary party posted nothing; aggregators posted after the wires.

Three points for the primary source, none for newsletters, none for timing: 3/10.

Stakes 9/10. A licensing rule changes supervision for a class of exports and binds five countries at once; only a statute would put it beyond the next administration's discretion.

Thesis

A control that is announced and not enforced is a signal, not a control. For eighteen months Washington named the prohibited sales, and the sales continued. The capacity they paid for is installed, and no licence reaches it. Diffusion of compute now runs at the speed of foreign policy: the state's hesitation is a larger variable than the price of a chip.

Antithesis

The delay is ordinary administration: an incoming government may withdraw its predecessor's rule, and this one did so openly and in advance. The real constraint was the factory, not the border, because supply and price decided where clusters stood. Reuters documents a channel, not a volume. The strongest objection is constitutional: an interim final rule binds before comment closes, so the discretion that opened the gap can widen it again.

Forecast

By 30 June 2027 the Bureau of Industry and Security will publish at least one enforcement action naming a data-centre operator in Singapore, Malaysia or Indonesia for diverting advanced AI chips to a customer headquartered in China. Probability 55 %. An order, settlement or denial letter counts; a general statement of concern does not. Resolves 30 June 2027.

Political consequences

1-2 years. The instrument is the licence condition in the interim final rule. The actor is the Bureau of Industry and Security, which must close the comment period and publish a final rule. The observable is a final rule with a published count of licences granted and denied.

3-4 years. The instrument needed is a statute that gives the regime a reporting duty and a penalty schedule. The actor is the Senate banking committee, which holds export administration. The observable is an enacted clause requiring an annual public count of licences by destination.

Market test: pro-business, and the protection of incumbents dominates. The instrument is a licence requirement on data-centre buyers in five countries, written at the direction of the Treasury and the National Security Council. The buyer with a legal team gains; a new entrant waits in a queue the incumbent never joins.

What to watch

  • Whether the final rule keeps the five destinations or adds the United Arab Emirates.
  • Whether the bureau publishes its first licence denial.
  • Whether any Chinese laboratory names the data centre that trained its last model.
  • The forecast on Anthropic's first results release resolves on 17 February 2027.
