6 November 2026 - Machine Learning Societies

Voters now price AI compute, and OpenAI writes its own permit

Signal 7/10 · Stakes 7/10

Voters decided 47 local measures on data centres on 3 November: 45 backed a restriction and one allowed construction. Two days later OpenAI published a compact to pay for grid upgrades, publish water and power data and end non-disclosure agreements with officials. The constraint on AI buildout has moved from capital to local consent.

Signal

Twenty-one measures were in Ohio. The tracker Data Centers on the Ballot counts 45 restricting results, one allowing result and one undecided. Officials must certify the counts.

Observed. Sunbury, Ohio, banned campuses above 25 megawatts with 78 % of the vote; Granville banned all data-centre construction with 82 %. In Piqua, Ohio, voters recalled the mayor and two council members over a Meta-backed project. Texas changed too: James Talarico won a Senate seat and Gina Hinojosa the governor's race; Democrats took the state House for the first time in 24 years. An NBC News exit poll found that 56 % of voters called data centres a crucial or major factor in their congressional vote. Democratic candidates spent more than

00 million on ads that tied opponents to data centres. Markets read the result as a slowdown: Microsoft rose 6.7 %, Amazon 6.1 % and Alphabet 3.3 % the next day. OpenAI answered on 5 November with a National Community Compact. It promises to pay the full cost of electricity and grid work, publish site water, power and noise data, avoid non-disclosure agreements with officials, and report hiring each year.

Caveats. A local vote is not a certified national statistic. The 45–1 count comes from a private tracker, other tallies give 43 of 44, and the undecided measure can change the total. Many measures are advisory, temporary or written for one project. State legislatures can preempt local zoning, and a court can strike a charter amendment. The compact is voluntary, covers only OpenAI-owned sites and carries no penalty. Investors read the vote as slower spending, not as a stop.

Assessment. The useful number is not 45. It is the cost of consent. A 25-megawatt threshold is now the line at which a hyperscale campus needs a local majority, and 45 communities have priced that majority at a referendum. For a reader outside AI: a data centre is no longer an industrial project approved in a hearing room. It is a political campaign with a budget. The constraint has moved from transformers and turbines to the ballot box. OpenAI's compact is the first attempt to write the permit before a legislature does. The stakes are 7 because the permitting standard now changes across a sector.

Evidence 7/10.

  • P. County tallies and OpenAI's compact are primary records: vote counts and the company's own commitments. 3 points.
  • N. MIT Technology Review named the Texas races. The Deep View reported the exit poll and the ad sum. Axios reported the rally with named price moves. 3 points.
  • L. OpenAI published the compact before the newsletters. 1 point.

The sum is 3 + 3 + 1 = 7.

Stakes 7/10. The votes change a planning rule for every developer in the sector, and the compact sets a private standard that rivals must answer.

Thesis

AI buildout is now a permitting problem, and permission is won locally. The ballot box has become the siting authority for compute. A laboratory can buy chips, land and power. It cannot buy a majority in a town of 9,000. OpenAI's compact is an attempt to answer that fact with a private permit. The scarce resource is a host community that says yes.

Antithesis

The strongest objection is that a local vote is not a national rule. Forty-five small towns do not stop a hyperscaler that can move to another county. State legislatures can preempt them. The compact covers only campuses OpenAI owns; Oracle and other partners own much of the capacity. A voluntary standard with no penalty is marketing until a buyer adopts it. The market read the election as bullish for Big Tech, which suggests investors expect the buildout to continue.

Forecast

By 30 June 2027, at least one US state legislature enacts a statute that limits local bans or moratoriums on data centres (55 %). Resolution date: 30 June 2027. Scoring rule: a hit requires an enacted statute that names data centres and limits a local restriction. A bill or a general statement on permitting does not count.

Political consequences

One to two years. Instrument: state preemption statutes and zoning law. Actor: the legislatures and governors of Ohio, Texas and Georgia. Observable: one enacted state law that limits local data-centre moratoriums or requires a host-community agreement.

Three to four years. Instrument: cost-allocation tariffs for large electricity loads. Actor: FERC and state utility commissions. Observable: a rate case that makes data centres pay for network upgrades, plus a public register of host-community payments.

Market test: pro-business, and that effect dominates. Instrument: a voluntary corporate compact beside local permitting; actor: OpenAI. The beneficiary is the incumbent with a legal team and a balance sheet.

What to watch

The certification of the undecided measure. The first state bill that would preempt a local data-centre ban. OpenAI's first annual report. And the nearest forecast in the ledger: on 31 January 2027, the group led by Impagliazzo, Paturi and Williams must publish its promised verdict.

How the Signal is scored

Every edition prints two numbers, and they are read together. The first measures the quality of the evidence. The second measures how much changes if the evidence holds.

Signal, 0–10: the evidence. The formula is E = 3P + N + L, and the maximum is ten.

  • P — primary source. Three points when the claim rests on the actor's own document, announcement or statement. Zero when it rests on reporting about the actor.
  • N — independent confirmation. One point for each newsletter or newsroom that adds a fact of its own. Repeating a press release adds nothing.
  • L — first publisher. One point when the party at the centre of the story published it first.

A story with a primary document, one independent outlet that adds a fact, and the actor as first publisher scores five. The same story repeated ten times without a new fact still scores four.

Stakes, 1–10: the consequence.

  • 1 — one team's experiment.
  • 5 — a workflow adopted across a sector.
  • 7 — a procurement rule or standard changes.
  • 10 — market structure changes.

The anchors are steps, not a scale of effort: 3 is a change inside one organisation, 9 is a rule that binds most of a market. A low signal with high stakes is a thin claim about a large thing, and it is marked as such.
