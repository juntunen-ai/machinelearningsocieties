28 November 2026 - Machine Learning Societies

Inference has a common scoreboard, and the ranking is a cost estimate

Signal 4/10 · Stakes 6/10

On 26 November the research firm SemiAnalysis put Google's TPU and Amazon's Trainium on InferenceX and AgentX, the public benchmark it runs for inference, the work of answering a request once a model is trained. It said the chips deliver 1.2 to 2.5 times the performance per dollar of Nvidia's processors. Amazon reports 2.28 times at the peak, yet its measured throughput per chip was only 4 % higher; the rest comes from a cost estimate of $1.05 per chip-hour against $2.31. AWS publishes no list price, so a buyer cannot check it.

Signal

Observed. The benchmark publishes its recipes in a public repository, and every point links to a GitHub Actions run that a reader can open. The hardware list names NVIDIA's GB300 NVL72 and Vera Rubin NVL72, AMD's MI355X, Google's TPU7x, Amazon's Trainium3 PDS and OpenAI's Jalapeño. AgentX replays recorded coding sessions with long shared context and parallel subagents, not single prompts.

Observed. The entry runs Z.ai's 744-billion-parameter GLM-5.2 and GLM-5.3 on vLLM in FP4 precision, and plots tokens per dollar against P90 interactivity, the speed that the slowest tenth of users see. Trainium3 leads by 1.98 times near 140 tokens per second per user, 2.28 times near 250 and 2.01 times near 350. SemiAnalysis calls the figures an official preview. Time to first token is 4.5 seconds at P90, against 1.8 seconds for GB300.

Caveats. The $1.05 is a model of ownership cost at hyperscaler volume, not a rental price. It assumes a depreciation schedule, a power price and a utilisation rate that the dashboard does not publish together. The benchmark's own page says participants often submit software images built for the test. Trade reports say the order reverses on its internal cost basis, with Vera Rubin 3.3 times ahead. A ranking that moves with the accounting basis measures the basis.

Assessment. Until this week the price of an AI feature could be compared only across suppliers that published a price, and custom chips had no public number. A finance officer can now ask a supplier to quote against a published figure. What would change the decision is a list price and a latency that fits the service: 4.5 seconds suits batch work and rules out a chat window. Stakes are 6 because inference is a budget line in every organisation that runs a model.

Evidence 4/10.

  • P: the public repository, the dashboard entry and the pull request that added it. Three points.
  • N: no independent newsletter added a fact; the one that covered it belongs to the benchmark's owner. No points.
  • L: SemiAnalysis posted the entry on X on 26 November, before the trade coverage of 27 November. One point.

The sum is 3 + 0 + 1 = 4.

Stakes 6/10. Inference is a line in the operating budget of every organisation that serves a model to users, and the benchmark now sets the comparison for that line.

Thesis

Running a model has become the cost of the product. A benchmark that reports tokens per dollar therefore does political work: it decides which supplier looks expensive. This week that judgement rests on an estimate of depreciation, power price and utilisation that the publisher writes alone. The supplier that wins published no price, and the firm that publishes the ranking sells research to the same buyers.

Antithesis

Buyers do not buy benchmarks. A cloud customer signs for rented hours, and the same dashboard lists rental prices beside ownership costs. Firms that serve inference at scale test their own traffic: prompt lengths, cache hit rates, promised latency. On that test, a 4 % speed difference and a 2.2 times cost difference are what a buyer should weigh, and public logs are more checkable than a datasheet. If the cost model is wrong, AWS loses the rentals.

Forecast

By 30 June 2027 Amazon will publish a public hourly price for Trainium3 capacity that an outside buyer can check against the $1.05 per chip-hour estimate. Probability 60 %. Resolves 30 June 2027. A page that says only "contact sales", a partner quote, or a price for another chip is a miss.

Political consequences

1-2 years. The instrument is the framework agreement. Public buyers in the EU purchase compute through EuroHPC and national central purchasing bodies, and those documents set the criteria a supplier must meet. The actor is the central purchasing body. The observable is a tender that names an inference benchmark, or a tokens-per-euro figure, as an award criterion.

3-4 years. The instrument needed is a disclosure duty for performance claims used in public purchasing, for example in a harmonised standard under the accuracy and robustness requirement of the AI Act. The actor is CEN-CENELEC with the AI Office. The observable is a standard that requires a supplier to publish the configuration, the cost basis and the date beside any performance claim.

Market test: mixed, and the cost model dominates. The pro-market instrument is the open repository with public logs, which lets a new chip entrant be compared on the same recipe. The pro-business instrument is the unpublished cost basis: the publisher sets the order, the winner gains a number without a price, and no buyer can reproduce it.

What to watch

  • Whether Amazon publishes a list price for Trainium3 capacity.
  • Whether SemiAnalysis publishes the inputs to the $1.05 model.
  • Whether the preliminary label comes off, and which numbers change.
  • Whether any EU, Nordic or Finnish tender names an inference benchmark in its award criteria.
  • Forecast resolutions due within 90 days: 31 January 2027, 6 February 2027, 17 February 2027.
