The Fed names AI debt as a stability risk

Signal 7/10 · Stakes 9/10

The Federal Reserve's semiannual Financial Stability Report, published on 13 November, puts artificial intelligence at the top of its risk list. Eighty-seven per cent of surveyed market contacts name AI as a salient risk, against 50 per cent in May. The report also counts the borrowing that pays for the buildout: USD 680 billion of advanced-computing debt by October.

Signal

Observed. The Federal Reserve released its Financial Stability Report on Friday 13 November. The New York Fed polled 23 professionals at banks, funds and advisory firms in September and October. A risk-asset correction came second at 52 per cent, long-term rates at 48 per cent, and cyberattacks and fiscal debt at 43 per cent each. The report names three AI channels: high equity valuations, the risk of a cyberattack by a state actor or by a frontier model, and complex AI-related financing.

The financing numbers carry the weight. Advanced-computing debt issuance reached USD 680 billion by October, about five times the whole of 2023. It now makes up nearly a third of all nonfinancial investment-grade corporate bond issuance, against less than 6 per cent three years ago. The Fed estimates that debt, guarantees and leases not yet started could exceed USD 3 trillion. The largest US bank holding companies hold close to USD 1 trillion of exposure to large technology firms, data-centre developers, special purpose vehicles, private credit funds and utilities. Hedge fund leverage is the highest since the Fed began collecting the data in 2013.

Caveats. The 87 per cent is a sentiment reading from 23 respondents, not a supervisory finding. The Fed states that the survey reflects the views of contacts, not those of the Board or the New York Fed. The report also argues that large firms can absorb losses inside the circular structure, which limits contagion.

Assessment. The report moves AI credit from a market story to an object of official monitoring. It names the instruments that carry the risk, namely leases, guarantees and special purpose vehicles, and it states that balance sheets understate the exposure. A bank supervisor who writes that sentence has opened a file. The stakes are 9/10 because the next step is a supervision rule: a data collection, a risk weight or a disclosure duty for compute-backed credit.

Evidence 7/10.

  • P — 3 points: Primary record: the Federal Reserve Financial Stability Report of 13 November 2026.
  • N — 3 points: Three newsrooms add a fact of their own. Reuters reports the USD 3 trillion estimate and the bank exposure. CNBC reports the hedge fund leverage. MT Newswires reports the 23 respondents and the survey method.
  • L — 1 point: The Fed published the report before the coverage.

The sum is P 3 + N 3 + L 1 = 7/10.

Stakes 9/10.

The next step is a supervision rule that changes how banks hold and report the exposure.

Thesis

The buildout no longer runs on equity alone. It runs on leases, guarantees and project debt, arranged by firms that also buy the output. That structure turns a technology bet into a credit exposure held by banks, insurers and private funds. Once a central bank counts the exposure in its stability report, the constraint on AI capacity stops being chip supply and becomes the lender's balance sheet and the supervisor's data request. Capital still flows, but at a price that carries a named risk.

Antithesis

The strongest objection is that a survey is not a rule. Twenty-three respondents produce a headline, not a capital requirement, and the Fed disclaims the result as its own view. The report argues the opposite of alarm: hyperscalers can absorb losses, so the structure may dampen contagion. A third of investment-grade issuance sounds large until the contracts behind it are read, because long-term compute contracts with investment-grade buyers resemble utility revenue bonds more than venture debt. The 1990s comparison ends in overbuilt fibre at low prices, not in a banking crisis.

Forecast

Horizon: through 30 June 2027. By that date, the Federal Reserve or another G7 central bank will name AI-related credit as a monitored vulnerability in a formal supervisory document and start a named data collection on it. Probability: 60 per cent. Resolution date: 30 June 2027. Score YES if a formal document and a named collection both exist; PARTIAL if only one does.

Market test — pro-business. Instrument: supervisory monitoring of compute-backed credit. Actors: the Federal Reserve Board, the Financial Stability Oversight Council and the bank supervisors. Who gains: hyperscalers and banks that can guarantee their own projects, at the expense of entrants that must rent capacity.

Political consequences

1-2 years: Instrument: a supervisory data collection on leases, guarantees and special purpose vehicles; actors: the Federal Reserve Board and the Financial Stability Oversight Council; observable: a new reporting line for compute-backed exposure in bank filings before the end of 2027.

3-4 years: Instrument: a risk weight or disclosure rule for compute-backed project debt; actors: the Basel Committee and the Securities and Exchange Commission; observable: a published capital or disclosure treatment that names data-centre leases.

What to watch

  • Whether the fourth-quarter bank filings disclose special purpose vehicle exposure by name.
  • Whether the next Financial Stability Report keeps the same three AI channels.
  • Whether Anthropic prices its listing on Thursday at a valuation that assumes continued cheap credit.
How the Signal is scored

Every edition prints two numbers, and they are read together. The first measures the quality of the evidence. The second measures how much changes if the evidence holds.

Signal, 0–10: the evidence. The formula is E = 3P + N + L, and the maximum is ten.

  • P — primary source. Three points when the claim rests on the actor's own document, announcement or statement. Zero when it rests on reporting about the actor.
  • N — independent confirmation. One point for each newsletter or newsroom that adds a fact of its own. Repeating a press release adds nothing.
  • L — first publisher. One point when the party at the centre of the story published it first.

Stakes, 1–10: the consequence. 1 is one team's experiment. 3 is one workflow in one organisation. 5 is a workflow adopted across a sector. 7 is a procurement rule or standard that changes. 9 is a statute, a liability rule or a supervision rule. 10 is a change in market structure. The even steps are the cases between them.
