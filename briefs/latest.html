Self-improvement gets a number, a filing duty, and no shared metric

Signal 4/10 · Stakes 8/10

Two members of the United States House introduced a bill on 24 November that would make the largest AI developers report each quarter on how much of their research AI performs. The same day OpenAI published the pretraining loss of thirteen models it trained since GPT-3.5, and METR found the loss fell about seven times faster after February 2026. The bill asks who must report, the curve asks what can be reported, and neither settles the unit.

Signal

Observed. On 24 November Representatives Josh Gottheimer (Democrat, New Jersey) and Nathaniel Moran (Republican, Texas) introduced the RSI Safety Act. It covers recursive self-improvement, the process by which a model trains its successor. A covered developer would report each quarter to a new Office of Frontier Artificial Intelligence Research at the Department of Commerce. The report would state the share of research that AI performs and the share that a person reviews. The act applies to a developer that trains a model above a compute threshold and spent at least $100 million on training in the past year.

The office would publish aggregate reports and issue guidance on auditable metrics. The act protects whistleblowers and allows liability of up to $10 million for a false or missing report. On the same day OpenAI published the base-model log-perplexity of thirteen models since GPT-3.5, measured on one fixed held-out dataset from its own codebase. METR placed a breakpoint in February 2026. The slope moved from about 0.022 a year to about 0.156, 7.1 times steeper, so the three-year fall from GPT-3.5 to GPT-4.1 would repeat in about six and a half months. Microsoft published a comparable curve on the same dataset.

Caveats. The bill is introduced, not enacted, and it has no hearing date. The acceleration rests on one corpus from the developer’s own code. METR names three ways it can mislead: overfitting to that corpus, a shrinking gap between pretraining and release, and the absence of a second dataset. The curve measures how well a model predicts that codebase’s text. It does not show that the model plans or verifies better.

Assessment. This is the first legislative text about machines that improve themselves, and it arrived on the day the curve such a text needs became public. The bill asks who must report. The curve asks what can be reported. Neither settles the unit. A filing that says AI performed 40 % of the research means little without a defined task, a defined reviewer and an audited corpus. The stakes reach 8 because the text creates a supervision duty with a named office and a $10 million liability. Only enactment separates it from law.

Evidence 4/10.

  • P: 1 — the bill as reported by Politico, the METR note of 24 November, and the loss values that OpenAI published.
  • N: 0 — no newsletter in the monitored inbox covered either event before this edition.
  • L: 1 — METR published its analysis before the newsletters of the next morning.

Three points for the primary source, none for newsletters, one for timing: 4/10.

Stakes 8/10. The act changes supervision for every developer above the compute and spending thresholds, and it attaches a liability of $10 million to the filing. Enactment alone separates it from level 9.

Thesis

The political question changed in one day. It is no longer whether to permit self-improvement. It is which number the state accepts, who audits the corpus, and which firms fall outside the threshold. A disclosure duty is the cheapest instrument on the shelf. That is why the metric becomes the political object: the labs that published a curve chose the data and the models.

Antithesis

The bill may be a press release with a legislative number. A bipartisan pair filed it in a chamber that ends in January 2027, with no hearing date. The $100 million threshold exempts every startup and every European lab. The sharper instruments this month came from a state and an amendment: Governor Newsom’s framework and Representative Trahan’s auditing text name a test, not a form. A fixed compliance cost also favours the incumbent that already employs the lawyers.

Forecast

By 31 May 2027 at least one frontier developer other than OpenAI and Microsoft will publish a pretraining-loss curve for three or more of its own models, measured on one fixed held-out dataset. Probability 55 %. A blog post, paper or executive post by the developer counts; a third-party estimate does not. Fewer than three models counts as a miss.

Political consequences

1-2 years. The instrument is the quarterly reporting duty in the RSI Safety Act. The actor is the House committee that receives the text, and it must schedule a hearing or a markup before January 2027. The observable is a published hearing notice, a markup, or a named director for the new office.

3-4 years. The instrument needed is a statute that names the metric and the corpus, not only the duty to report. The actor is the Senate committee on commerce and science, which must write a companion text. The observable is an enacted clause that requires a third-party held-out dataset, or a procurement rule that makes the disclosure a condition of a federal contract.

Market test: mixed, and the pro-business effect dominates. The instrument is a quarterly filing to a new office, and the actors are the two sponsors with the Commerce Department. The threshold draws the line at scale, and the measurement rules are written with a standards centre where the incumbents already advise.

What to watch

  • Whether a House committee schedules a hearing or a markup on the RSI Safety Act before January 2027.
  • Whether a second lab publishes a loss curve on a corpus that it does not own.
  • Whether the standards centre’s guidance names pretraining loss at all.
  • The forecast on Anthropic’s first results release resolves on 17 February 2027.
