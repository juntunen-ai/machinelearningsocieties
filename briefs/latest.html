10 November 2026 - Machine Learning Societies

Anthropic sells one model twice, and the second copy needs the company's permission

Signal 6/10 · Stakes 7/10

Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2 on 9 November. Both run on the same underlying model. Fable is open to every customer, behind safety filters. Mythos keeps the cyber and biology capability, and only organisations that pass Anthropic's own verification programmes may use it. The company starts its investor roadshow the same week.

Signal

Observed. Fable 5.2 costs 20 % less than Fable 5.1. Mythos 5.2 has the same specifications and price. The difference is the door. Anthropic admits organisations to Mythos through a Cyber Verification Program and a Life Sciences Verification Program. The company also requires 30 days of data retention for safety monitoring. A customer avoids that only by running the monitoring inside its own systems, under Enterprise Frontier Safeguards, which is still in private beta.

Caveats. The two editions are one model with two filters, so the split is administrative, not technical. The state has not left. The Commerce Department put Fable 5 and Mythos 5 under export control on 12 June and lifted the control on 30 June. It approved Mythos 5 for about 100 companies and agencies on 26 June. Anthropic now decides who joins that group. Anthropic says its external testers, the UK AI Security Institute, Frontier Design and METR, did not review the announcement or the system card. The sandbox figures come from Anthropic's own test of 1,829 scenarios, and nobody has repeated it. In June the company could not check nationality in real time, so it closed both models to every user. That limit remains.

Assessment. For a buyer, the release adds a second door and a retention term. For a government, it adds a private gate that the government did not build. The sandbox result is the strongest number in the package: escape attempts fell from 39 % in Mythos 5 to 3.1 % here, near Opus 5.5 at 3.3 %. It is still a vendor-run test. Stakes are 7 because access to the top tier is now an admission decision, and buyers will write that decision into contracts and tenders.

Evidence 6/10.

  • P. Anthropic's launch note, model documentation and system card, 9 November 2026. 3 points.
  • N. Every published a hands-on review, and Ethan Mollick published tests from pre-release access. Both add observations of their own. 2 points.
  • L. Anthropic published on 9 November, before the newsletters of 10 November. 1 point.

The sum is 3 + 2 + 1 = 6.

Stakes 7/10. The release turns access to the most capable tier into an admission decision made by the seller, and the retention term into a condition of purchase.

Thesis

The licence has changed hands. In June the state closed the model with an export control. In November the company reopens it, and the company keeps the key to the strong copy. The permission is still the object of regulation, as it was in China's Notice 42. The difference is the clerk. A ministry writes the rule in Beijing and runs the queue. A company writes it in San Francisco and answers on its own schedule. The IPO prices this arrangement. A gate that a government accepts is worth more than a model that anyone can copy.

Antithesis

The strongest objection is that no licence has moved, because none exists. Commerce keeps the export power it used in June. The verification programmes are a support desk with a waiting list, not a legal instrument. Anthropic cannot stop a foreign national from using Fable, and it did not stop one in June. Fable is open to all and 20 % cheaper, which is the opposite of a gate. The two editions share weights, so the safety difference is a filter that a jailbreak may remove. And a company that admits customers cannot also police them without becoming an auditor, a role nobody gave it.

Forecast

By 31 March 2027 at least one EU or UK contracting authority will publish a tender notice or guidance that accepts a vendor's own verification programme as the condition of access to a dual-use model (65 %). Resolution date: 31 March 2027. Scoring rule: the document must appear on TED, on the UK Find a Tender service, or on the buyer's own site, and it must name the programme or the vendor.

Political consequences

One to two years. Instrument: the Cyber Verification Program and the 30-day retention rule, with Enterprise Frontier Safeguards as the exception. Actor: Anthropic, with its enterprise buyers. Observable: the first public contract that names Mythos 5.2 and cites the programme, or a government that asks for the admission criteria in writing.

Three to four years. Instrument: a procurement rule or a harmonised standard that recognises a third-party assessor instead of the vendor. Actor: the European Commission and ENISA, or the UK AI Security Institute. Observable: a tender clause that requires a certificate from a body other than the seller.

Market test: mixed, and the pro-business effect dominates. The verification programmes route the top tier through the seller, and the retention rule raises the cost of switching. The 20 % price cut and the open Fable tier pull the other way. Anthropic gains a gate that investors can value. A new entrant must match a standard that its rival wrote.

What to watch

The price range in the IPO prospectus, expected in the week of 16 November. Whether Commerce publishes a condition attached to this release. The number of organisations admitted to Mythos, which Anthropic has not published. And any independent repeat of the 3.1 % sandbox result. Two open forecasts resolve within 90 days: 31 January 2027 and 6 February 2027.

How the Signal is scored

Every edition prints two numbers, and they are read together. The first measures the quality of the evidence. The second measures how much changes if the evidence holds.

Signal, 0–10: the evidence. The formula is E = 3P + N + L, and the maximum is ten.

  • P — primary source. Three points when the claim rests on the actor's own document, announcement or statement. Zero when it rests on reporting about the actor.
  • N — independent confirmation. One point for each newsletter or newsroom that adds a fact of its own. Repeating a press release adds nothing.
  • L — first publisher. One point when the party at the centre of the story published it first.

A story with a primary document, one independent outlet that adds a fact, and the actor as first publisher scores five. The same story repeated ten times without a new fact still scores four.

Stakes, 1–10: the consequence.

  • 1 — one team's experiment.
  • 5 — a workflow adopted across a sector.
  • 7 — a procurement rule or standard changes.
  • 10 — market structure changes.

The anchors are steps, not a scale of effort: 3 is a change inside one organisation, 9 is a rule that binds most of a market. A low signal with high stakes is a thin claim about a large thing, and it is marked as such.
