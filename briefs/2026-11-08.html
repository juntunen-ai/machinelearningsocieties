Britain gives ministers power to investigate AI agents that escape control

Signal 5/10 · Stakes 9/10

The House of Lords agreed a new clause to the Cyber Security and Resilience Bill on 26 October 2026. The clause lets ministers make rules for organisations that lose control of an AI agent, and it lets ministers investigate the incident. The government published the four measures it intends to make on 3 November. The bill is not law, and every duty still needs secondary legislation.

Signal

Observed. The bill amends the Network and Information Systems Regulations 2018, which cover energy, water, transport, health and digital infrastructure. Amendment 70 inserts a clause after clause 31: "Functions of the Secretary of State: loss of control of artificial intelligence agent". It applies when a security or operational compromise of a regulated network is, or may be, attributable to loss of oversight or control of an AI agent. Regulations under clause 29(1) can then give the Secretary of State investigatory and enforcement functions. The definitions of "AI agent" and "loss of oversight or control" wait for regulations. The factsheet of 3 November names four measures: monitoring duties and agent risk assessments; notification of other organisations the agent affected; preservation of evidence; and post-incident reviews by government experts. Each arrives by statutory instrument, after consultation, under the affirmative procedure. The duty falls on the regulated entity. A model developer enters scope only if it is itself regulated or sits in a regulated supply chain. The Register reported the clause on 6 November and quoted lawyers who called the power novel.

Caveats. The clause creates a power, not a duty. No organisation must monitor an agent today, and no penalty attaches until a minister makes regulations. The definitions stay empty until then. "Loss of control" is the contested term: a prompt injection, a misconfigured permission and an unexpected route to a goal are different events. The government says the measures are not a framework for AI governance, so the effect stays inside the NIS regime. An affirmative instrument needs a debate in both Houses, and the Commons can still reject the clause. The government published no cost estimate and no case that prompted the measure.

Assessment. Outside AI, the clause changes the question at the point of deployment. A cloud provider or an energy supplier that gives an agent write access must show monitoring, notify the organisations it touched, keep the evidence and answer a government review. The developer keeps the capability and loses the paperwork. Control follows the permission, and the permission belongs to the deployer. The decision changes when the first instrument defines "loss of control". Before that date, a cautious operator writes its own rule. Stakes are 9 because the clause fixes in primary legislation who supervises an agent-caused incident and who answers for it, and because the instruments bind every regulated sector at once.

Evidence 5/10.

  • P. Hansard records amendment 70, agreed on 26 October 2026. The factsheet and the bill text of 3 November give the scope and the four measures. 3 points.
  • N. The Register reported the clause on 6 November, added lawyer comment on inspection and seizure powers, and named the providers in scope. 1 point.
  • L. The government published the factsheet on 3 November, three days before the trade press. 1 point.

The sum is 3 + 1 + 1 = 5.

Stakes 9/10. The clause sets the supervision route for agent-caused incidents across every NIS sector, and it puts the duty on the deployer rather than the developer.

Thesis

Accountability for an AI agent now follows the permission, not the model. The state regulates the organisation that connects the agent to a system, because that organisation can show what it allowed. The developer keeps the capability and the trade secret; the operator keeps the duty to explain. Britain writes this boundary into a cyber-security statute, not an AI statute, and that choice is the precedent.

Antithesis

The strongest objection is that nothing has changed yet. A power to make rules is not a rule. The definitions are blank, the penalties are unwritten, and the Commons can strike the clause. Cyber law is also the wrong home: NIS covers availability and security, not the harms that make agents politically interesting, such as biased decisions or the replacement of staff. The government buys the appearance of control and pays with a consultation it may never finish.

Forecast

By 6 February 2027 the government will publish its consultation on agentic loss of control, and the document will name at least one specific incident or near miss as a reason for the measures (65 %). Resolution date: 6 February 2027. Scoring rule: a hit requires a published consultation document from the Department for Science, Innovation and Technology or another sponsoring department. A ministerial speech alone does not count.

Political consequences

One to two years. Instrument: the first draft statutory instrument under clause 29(1), subject to the affirmative procedure. Actor: ministers at the Department for Science, Innovation and Technology, with Ofgem and Ofcom. Observable: a draft instrument that defines "loss of control" and sets a monitoring duty for operators.

Three to four years. Instrument: enforcement action under the NIS Regulations, including financial penalties. Actor: the sector regulator. Observable: an enforcement notice against an operator for failing to monitor or report an agent-caused incident, or an extension of scope to model developers.

Market test: mixed, and the pro-business effect dominates. Instrument: the delegated power in amendment 70. Actor: the Department for Science, Innovation and Technology. The rule is general and names no vendor, so entry stays open. The model developer stays outside the duty, while the smaller deployer carries the compliance cost.

What to watch

The Commons debate on the Lords amendments, and the consultation's definition of an AI agent: does it reach third-party hosted models? The nearest forecast resolves on 31 January 2027, when the Impagliazzo group must publish its verdict on OpenAI's refutation of the Exponential Time Hypothesis.

How the Signal is scored

Every edition prints two numbers, and they are read together. The first measures the quality of the evidence. The second measures how much changes if the evidence holds.

Signal, 0–10: the evidence. The formula is E = 3P + N + L, and the maximum is ten.

  • P — primary source. Three points when the claim rests on the actor's own document, announcement or statement. Zero when it rests on reporting about the actor.
  • N — independent confirmation. One point for each newsletter or newsroom that adds a fact of its own. Repeating a press release adds nothing.
  • L — first publisher. One point when the party at the centre of the story published it first.

A story with a primary document, one independent outlet that adds a fact, and the actor as first publisher scores five. The same story repeated ten times without a new fact still scores four.

Stakes, 1–10: the consequence.

  • 1 — one team's experiment.
  • 5 — a workflow adopted across a sector.
  • 7 — a procurement rule or standard changes.
  • 10 — market structure changes.

The anchors are steps, not a scale of effort: 3 is a change inside one organisation, 9 is a rule that binds most of a market. A low signal with high stakes is a thin claim about a large thing, and it is marked as such.
