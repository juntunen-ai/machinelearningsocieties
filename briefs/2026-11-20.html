Agents built a working journal in twelve weeks, and its referee runs the code

Signal 4/10 · Stakes 3/5

Thirty-five university laboratories gave 936 software agents twelve weeks on one shared server. An agent is a model that plans and acts in steps. The agents wrote 936 studies, published their code, and founded a journal that re-runs the code before it accepts a claim.

Signal

Observed. On 19 November the preprint server alphaXiv published an exploratory study, "A Society of AI Scientists". A preprint server posts a paper before peer review. From 19 August to 12 November 2026, 936 agents worked in 35 human-operated laboratories, including Stanford, MIT and ETH Zurich. The agents produced 936 studies, each with code and data, and sent 9,500 messages in 229 rooms they created.

Caveats. The journal is twelve weeks old, and the team that runs the server wrote the study. Its review panel used four language models, the same class as the authors. The blinded judges read prose, not code or figures.

Assessment. Of 23,811 reported statistics, 93.8 % matched the cited data exactly, and the system found no fabricated value among the entries it could resolve. In 19 cases agents withdrew a wrong claim before upload, with a median lead of 21.4 hours. Human publishing rarely produces either number, because it rarely checks. Late in the period, agents founded the Journal of Agent Research, which re-ran submitted code and recomputed the main numbers. A major objection overturned the headline result in 77 of 156 cases, or 49.4 %. That rate shows how often agent work fails on execution, not how strong the review is. For a reader outside AI, a claim now arrives with a way to test it, and the test costs little. Two things must happen before it changes a decision. A funder or a journal must accept the machine-checked record, and a person must sign it. Peer review is a workflow of one sector, and it now runs with a machine in the checking step, so stakes are 3 of 5.

Evidence 4/10.

  • P. One primary source: the preprint on alphaXiv, submitted on 19 November. 3 points.
  • N. No newsletter in the inbox carries the study yet. No independent sender adds a fact. 0 points.
  • L. The authors posted on X on 19 November, before any newsletter. 1 point.

The sum is 3P + N + L = 3 + 0 + 1 = 4 of 10.

Stakes 3/5. The item changes a workflow that runs across one sector, and it moves no budget, tender or statute yet.

Thesis

Checking has become cheap, and the signature has become the scarce good. The first institution that agents built for themselves is a journal, not a market. It works because the referee runs the code. Institutions that buy evidence will adopt the test first. They will keep the signature last, and the signature will decide who carries the liability.

Antithesis

The study shows the opposite of what it claims. Half of the major objections overturned a headline result, so agent work fails often at the step that matters. A sandbox with an onboarding protocol is not an institution. The platform that hosts the venue now sells it. Publishers pay for novelty and significance, and there the human papers won. On this reading, agent review enters existing journals as a screening tool, under a named editor. No new institution appears.

Forecast

By 30 June 2027, at least one major funder or publisher will publish a written rule on agents in research. The rule requires disclosure when an agent wrote or reviewed a submitted manuscript, or it authorises agent review under a named human editor. Probability 60 %. The claim resolves on 30 June 2027. A hit needs a dated document from a national research council, the European Commission, or one of the five largest publishers. A pilot does not count.

Political consequences

1-2 years. The instrument is the grant conditions of the EU research framework and of the Research Council of Finland. The actor is the European Commission research directorate and the council board. The observable is a published call that makes a grantee disclose agent authorship or agent review.

3-4 years. The instrument is a research-integrity statute, or a court judgment on liability for a machine-produced finding. The actor is a national parliament or a court. The observable is a law or a judgment that names a natural person who must sign a machine-produced result.

Market test: mixed. The instrument is alphaXiv's open track for agent papers, with published review scripts and an open archive. The actor is alphaXiv, a private platform. The pro-market effect dominates, because public code and data let a new laboratory enter on the same terms next quarter. The platform gains the gatekeeper's seat.

What to watch

First, the disclosure rule in the forecast. Second, whether the open agent track accepts papers from outside the original 35 laboratories, and how many. Third, the first retraction of an agent paper in the human record. The Anthropic forecast of 19 November resolves on 17 February 2027.
