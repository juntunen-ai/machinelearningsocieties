7 November 2026 - Machine Learning Societies

AI now does part of AI research, and the proof is redacted

Signal 7/10 · Stakes 6/10

METR, an independent evaluator, worked inside Anthropic from 12 August to 29 October 2026. It concludes that Anthropic now improves its models on well-specified tasks at about twice the rate of March 2024 to October 2025. It also concludes that AI itself likely causes a majority of that gain. Anthropic removed most of the evidence behind the finding.

Signal

METR published the assessment on 5 November. Anthropic opened its pretraining, training-compute and benchmark data, and its reinforcement learning (a method that rewards good answers). Two and a half METR staff worked for eleven weeks on Anthropic devices, with free model tokens and no payment.

Observed. Scores on AECI, Anthropic's own capability index, grew at 1.9 times the rate recorded before October 2025, with a 95 % interval of 1.5 to 2.8 times. METR reads that as about two years of historical progress in one year. METR is confident that faster growth in training compute does not explain the result. It reports no clear bottleneck in algorithms, compute or data that would stop the trend within 12 months. Staff surveys put their own speed-up from the newest model at 12 times in October 2026. Anthropic redacted most of the material that METR found most informative. The public text summarises a full report of about 100 pages. Anthropic said the cuts remove information that could accelerate other developers.

Caveats. The two-times figure is a judgement, not a measurement. METR says the true rate could be 1.3 or 3. AECI is Anthropic's own index, and no one has shown how its scores map to useful work. The staff surveys are low-effort polls. The strongest evidence sits behind the redactions, so an outside reader cannot test it. The report covers one company, and it does not measure output, revenue or safety.

Assessment. The machine that writes code now helps to build the next machine, and the lab decides what the public may see of that loop. The question is no longer whether models can speed up research, but who may check the claim. The redaction right turns an evaluation into a transfer of trust: the public must believe METR, because it cannot read the evidence. The stakes are 6 because the practice now takes a contract and a budget line in the sector. A lab pays for evaluation time, and an evaluator accepts redaction terms. When the EU pays for the same work, the workflow becomes public infrastructure.

Evidence 7/10.

  • P. METR's report is a primary document with dates, methods and a redaction log. 3 points.
  • N. TLDR AI carried the finding with the report link. Generative AI reported the same doubling. The Deep View reported the redaction dispute and linked METR's own post. 3 points.
  • L. METR published on 5 November, one day before the newsletters. 1 point.

The sum is 3 + 3 + 1 = 7.

Stakes 6/10. The workflow changes across the sector: high-access evaluation with redaction rights, paid by the lab, and the EU now funds the same function.

Thesis

Frontier progress has become self-referential. The lab's own models supply a growing share of the research that produces the next model, and the evidence for that loop sits in private hands. Accountability therefore moves from publication to access. The scarce institutional good is a contract that lets an outsider read the data and speak in public.

Antithesis

The strongest objection is that the number is soft. A judgement with a range of 1.3 to 3, resting on a company's own index and on self-reports, cannot carry a claim about the pace of a field. Algorithmic progress was fast before AI wrote code, and better tooling, more compute and better data explain much of it. Redaction also protects real security interests in a race. On this reading, the public learns little it can check.

Forecast

By 30 June 2027, the European AI Office will publish qualification requirements for independent external evaluators of general-purpose models with systemic risk, and the document will give the evaluator a stated right of access to evidence, with redactions limited to named categories (55 %). Resolution date: 30 June 2027. Scoring rule: a hit requires a published Commission or AI Office document that sets the qualifications and states an access right. A workshop report alone does not count.

Political consequences

One to two years. Instrument: the Commission's call to increase EU evaluation capacity, with the AI Office's requirements for qualified external evaluators. Actor: the AI Office in DG CNECT. Observable: a signed framework contract that pays an evaluator to inspect provider evidence.

Three to four years. Instrument: the documentation and access powers in the AI Act, used through market-surveillance decisions. Actor: the Commission and national authorities. Observable: an enforcement decision that treats refusal of evaluator access as non-compliance.

Market test: mixed, and the pro-business effect dominates today. Instrument: the redaction right in the evaluation contract; actor: Anthropic. The incumbent keeps an information advantage that a new entrant cannot buy, while the public capacity that would correct it is still unfunded.

What to watch

The terms of METR's next access agreement. The AI Office's qualification document. And the nearest forecast: on 31 January 2027, the group led by Impagliazzo, Paturi and Williams must publish its promised verdict.

How the Signal is scored

Every edition prints two numbers, and they are read together. The first measures the quality of the evidence. The second measures how much changes if the evidence holds.

Signal, 0–10: the evidence. The formula is E = 3P + N + L, and the maximum is ten.

  • P — primary source. Three points when the claim rests on the actor's own document, announcement or statement. Zero when it rests on reporting about the actor.
  • N — independent confirmation. One point for each newsletter or newsroom that adds a fact of its own. Repeating a press release adds nothing.
  • L — first publisher. One point when the party at the centre of the story published it first.

A story with a primary document, one independent outlet that adds a fact, and the actor as first publisher scores five. The same story repeated ten times without a new fact still scores four.

Stakes, 1–10: the consequence.

  • 1 — one team's experiment.
  • 5 — a workflow adopted across a sector.
  • 7 — a procurement rule or standard changes.
  • 10 — market structure changes.

The anchors are steps, not a scale of effort: 3 is a change inside one organisation, 9 is a rule that binds most of a market. A low signal with high stakes is a thin claim about a large thing, and it is marked as such.
