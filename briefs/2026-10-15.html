15 October 2026 - Machine Learning Societies

The Financial Stability Board writes the first common AI checklist for banks

Signal 4/10 · Stakes 7/10

On 15 October the FSB published Sound Practices for Responsible Adoption of Artificial Intelligence: twelve practices covering governance and lifecycle management, addressed to banks, insurers and their supervisors. They are non-binding and explicitly not an international standard.

Signal

Observed. Twelve practices span organisation-wide AI governance, the management of AI through its lifecycle, third-party risk and the treatment of material AI uses. The report tells institutions to fold them into existing risk, cyber and outsourcing frameworks rather than build a parallel one, applied in proportion to size, complexity and the materiality of the use.

Caveats. Non-binding, so nothing is enforceable and no supervisor must act. The practices describe what good looks like, not how to evidence it, and say little about model performance or data provenance.

Assessment. Supervisory expectation usually arrives before the rule: a common list becomes the reference in supervisory dialogue, and then in bank policy. Stakes 7: a standard changes for a sector, without a statute.

Evidence 4/10.

  • P 3. The FSB final report itself, published 15 October 2026.
  • N 1. One newsletter that summarised the report and named its twelve practices.
  • L 0. No earlier post by the FSB account preceding the publication.

3 + 1 + 0 = 4.

Stakes 7/10. A standard changes across one sector: banks and insurers will be assessed against these practices, and procurement of AI tools will start to ask for them.

Thesis

In finance, governance documents are the diffusion mechanism. A shared list of twelve practices lets smaller institutions buy a compliance package instead of inventing one, and lets vendors sell against it.

Antithesis

A non-binding list is cheap to sign and expensive to evidence. Third-party risk practice collides with vendor contracts that few banks can audit, so the checklist may document dependence rather than control it.

Forecast

By 31 December 2027 at least five EU supervisors reference the FSB practices in published guidance or supervisory questionnaires (65 %).

Political consequences

1–2 years. Instrument: supervisory questionnaires and guidance. Actor: ECB and national financial supervisory authorities. Observable: an inspection manual or questionnaire that cites the twelve practices.
3–4 years. Instrument: capital and operational-resilience rules. Actor: EBA and national regulators. Observable: an AI-related expectation written into operational-risk or outsourcing requirements.

What to watch

Whether the ECB or EBA cites the practices by name in 2027, and whether any bank publishes an AI governance statement built on them.

How the signal is scored: Evidence E = 3P + N + L, maximum ten, where P is a primary source, N counts distinct newsletter senders that add a fact, and L marks the primary party posting first.
