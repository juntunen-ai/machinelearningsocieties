Texas closed the on-site power loophole, and the delay moved into the permit queue

Signal 6/10 · Stakes 7/10

On 24 October the research firm SemiAnalysis counted six Texas data-centre campuses with plans for their own power. The six campuses measure 8.7 gigawatts in total. All six need an air permit from the Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ). Governor Greg Abbott stopped those permits on 21 September.

Signal

The letter of 21 September tells TCEQ to pause "the issuance of all permits related to data center projects". The pause lasts until the Electric Reliability Council of Texas (ERCOT) completes its review. The letter also stops every other state agency from approving a data-centre permit before the audit ends.

The earlier order of 3 August covered grid connections only. A campus that built its own plant could avoid that queue. The plant ran behind the meter, which means it served the campus without a grid connection. The September letter closes that route, because an air permit gates the power plant. Site work and permitted generation continue.

Observed. TCEQ's interim executive director, Steven Schar, answered Abbott on 19 October. He wrote that staff screen pending applications and pause every application under the directive. Public Citizen counted 16 permits that TCEQ issued for data-centre projects after 21 September. The count covers air, wastewater and stormwater permits. TCEQ suspended the permits and shut its online filing system, STEERS. Applicants must now deliver paper copies to agency staff. ERCOT presents its audit to the Public Utility Commission on 10 December. The audit covers more than 400 data centres.

Caveats. The 8.7 gigawatts measure campus size, not capacity under delay. Three of the six campuses hold permits for a first part of their generation. One campus filed for an expansion permit and waits. Two campuses filed nothing. An air permit gates the plant alone. A three-month pause becomes a delay only when a campus needs the plant before the review ends. The count comes from a research firm that sells an industry model, not from a state register. TCEQ said it approved no permits, and then suspended the ones that Public Citizen named.

Assessment. For someone outside AI, the buildout now waits on state environmental agencies. A quarter's delay costs money, but it does not stop the build. The decision that matters is the first air permit after 10 December. A second state that copies the air-permit hook would turn a Texas pause into a national pattern.

Evidence 6/10.

  • P = 3: Abbott's letter, TCEQ's reply and ERCOT's 10 December date.
  • N = 2: the Texas Tribune published TCEQ's letter, and Texas Public Radio reported the 16 permits and the filing shutdown.
  • L = 1: SemiAnalysis published the 8.7 gigawatt count, and no newsletter carried it first.

Three plus two plus one is six out of ten.

Stakes 7/10. A state rule gates 8.7 gigawatts across six campuses, and it sets a test for every other state.

Thesis

The cheap route around the grid queue has closed. A data centre that makes its own power still needs an air permit. That permit ties the build to a state agency, a public comment period and a local air rule. Capital and chips no longer set the schedule alone.

Antithesis

The pause is not a ban. Three campuses hold permits for their first generation, and site work continues. The 8.7 gigawatts measure campus size, not delayed capacity. ERCOT may finish in December, and the queue restarts. Gas turbines, transformers and grid equipment may bind before any permit does. A developer can also move to a state with a faster agency. That choice would make Texas weaker, not stronger.

Forecast

By 31 March 2027, at least one Texas campus will state publicly that the TCEQ pause delayed a generation unit (60 %). A company statement, an ERCOT filing or a TCEQ record resolves the claim.

Political consequences

In one to two years the instrument is Abbott's 21 September directive and the TCEQ air permit. The actor is the TCEQ commission. The observable is the first new air permit for on-site generation after 10 December.

In three to four years the instrument is the federal Clean Air Act and its New Source Review. Texas applies the review through its state implementation plan. The actor is the Environmental Protection Agency or a federal court. The observable is a first decision that treats on-site generation for a data centre as a major source of pollution.

Market test: mixed, pro-business dominant. The instrument is the TCEQ permit freeze, and the actor is Governor Abbott. Three campuses with permitted generation gain time over new entrants. Residents gain protection from grid costs. The entry barrier dominates.

What to watch

Whether TCEQ issues a new air permit for on-site generation before 10 December. Whether any campus names a delay in a public filing. The duller signal: a second state that copies the air-permit hook.

A watch item is not a forecast. It names the document, decision or number that would settle the question this edition raises, so the call can be checked when it appears rather than argued about afterwards.

How the signal is scored

Two numbers are given for every edition, and they are read together. The first measures how good the evidence is. The second measures how much changes if the evidence holds.

Signal, 0–10: the evidence. Written as E = 3P + N + L and capped at ten.

  • P — primary source. Three points when the claim rests on the actor's own document, filing or statement. Zero when it rests only on reporting about the actor.
  • N — independent confirmation. One point for each additional newsletter or outlet that adds a fact of its own. Reprinting the same press release adds nothing.
  • L — first mover. One point when the party at the centre of the story is the one that published it first.

A story carried by the actor's own document, confirmed by one independent outlet that adds a fact, and published first by the actor scores five. The same story repeated by ten outlets that add nothing still scores four.

Stakes, 1–10: the consequence.

  • 1 — a single team's experiment.
  • 5 — a workflow adopted across a sector.
  • 7 — a procurement rule or standard changing.
  • 10 — market structure changing.

The anchors are steps, not a scale of effort: 3 is a change inside one organisation, 9 is a rule that binds most of a market. High signal with low stakes is a well-evidenced event that does not travel. Low signal with high stakes is a thin claim about something large, and it is labelled as one.
